# Headline selection

Use this reference when converting a post or article into cover copy.

## Extract the editorial promise

Write down four private notes before drafting:

- **Reader:** who should stop scrolling?
- **Payoff:** what useful change will reading create?
- **Tension:** what belief, mistake, risk, or missed opportunity creates curiosity?
- **Evidence boundary:** what does the source actually support?

Draft three to five distinct candidates rather than cosmetic rewrites. Useful angles include a concrete payoff, a costly mistake, a counterintuitive claim, a before/after change, or a precise question the article answers.

## Score and select

Score each candidate from 0–2 on every dimension:

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fidelity | Distorts the source | Broadly true | Precisely supported |
| Specificity | Empty or generic | Some context | Clear reader, object, or consequence |
| Curiosity | No reason to continue | Mild gap | Strong but honest information gap |
| Payoff | Benefit is absent | Implied | Immediately understood |
| Thumbnail readability | Too long or diffuse | Usable | Short, rhythmic, easy to scan |

Reject any candidate scoring 0 on fidelity, regardless of total. Prefer the highest score; use fidelity, then readability, as tie-breakers.

## Copy shape

- Prefer roughly 8–18 Chinese characters for the main headline; exceed this only when accuracy requires it.
- Use two or three deliberate lines, not automatic prose wrapping.
- Put one semantic unit on each line. Do not strand punctuation or split a fixed phrase.
- Emphasize one phrase with color or a paper block. If everything is emphasized, nothing is.
- Use at most one short supporting label when it contributes information the headline cannot carry.
- Preserve meaningful punctuation, Latin letters, numbers, and capitalization exactly.

Avoid empty superlatives, unsupported certainty, fake urgency, and vague hooks such as “太绝了” or “一定要看.”
