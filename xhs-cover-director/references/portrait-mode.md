# Portrait mode

Read this reference only when the user supplies a real-person photo.

## Source-of-truth rule

The uploaded portrait controls identity and geometry. Preserve recognizable facial features, face proportions, apparent age, skin tone, expression, gaze, pose, hand gesture, body proportions, clothing identity, and camera perspective. Never beautify by changing who the person appears to be.

Allowed default adjustments:

- exposure, white balance, contrast, and coherent color grading;
- restrained skin cleanup that leaves pores and natural texture;
- removal of small temporary distractions when it does not rewrite identity;
- subject cutout, a rough white sticker outline, and a soft separation shadow;
- background simplification or extension around existing negative space;
- subtle relighting consistent with the original light direction.

Do not default to face regeneration, facial reshaping, eye enlargement, body slimming, hairstyle replacement, new gestures, synthetic teeth, or expression changes.

## Composition from the original photo

Use pose and gaze as layout anchors:

- place copy in existing negative space first;
- let a pointing hand or gaze direct attention toward the headline or one key note;
- do not place text over eyes, mouth, hands, or an important object;
- if the portrait fills the frame, extend or simplify the background rather than shrinking the person unnaturally;
- keep the person large enough to retain emotional impact at thumbnail size.

Ask the image editor for a text-free result. Blank paper scraps and label shapes are allowed; pseudo-letters are not.

## Portrait quality gate

Compare the result with the source, not with an imagined ideal face. Check:

- identity and expression still read as the same person;
- eyes, teeth, ears, hairline, fingers, and clothing edges are coherent;
- skin retains texture and does not look waxy;
- the white outline follows the real silhouette without cutting off hair or fingers;
- lighting on subject and background belongs to one scene.
